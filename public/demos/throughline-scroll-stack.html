01 — Stages

Four stages that already know about each other

Most teams bolt a router, a vector store, an eval harness and a deploy script together and spend the next year maintaining the seams. These four share one config, one trace and one set of numbers.

01

Route

One endpoint in front of every model you use. Per-route budgets, automatic failover on 429 and 529, and a blended cost that updates as you change the mix.

p95 overhead — 41ms
02

Ground

Your corpus, chunked and embedded on ingest, queried with a floor you set. Every answer carries citations that resolve, or the answer does not leave the run.

Re-embed on write — no nightly job
03

Gate

An eval suite that runs on the diff, not on a schedule. Faithfulness, refusal rate, latency and cost, each with a threshold that blocks the deploy when it is missed.

240 cases — 3m 40s to green
04

Ship

Canary a percentage, watch the same four numbers in production, promote or roll back in one command. The rollback is a config change, so it takes seconds.

03 — Questions

The four we get in the first call

01 Do I have to move my prompts into your format?

No. The router takes the request you already send and returns what the provider returns; the only thing that changes is the base URL. Prompts, tools and streaming all pass through untouched, which is also what makes failover to a second model a config line rather than a rewrite.

02 What happens when a gate fails at 2am?

The deploy stops and the previous config keeps serving. Nothing pages until a human asks it to — the failing cases, their scores and the diff that caused them are waiting in the run when someone looks.

03 Where does my corpus live?

On Bench and Line, in an encrypted store in the region you pick, and you can export the chunks and their embeddings at any time. On Foundry it never leaves your network — the collector and the index run inside your VPC and we never see the text.

04 Is any of this real?

No. Throughline is a fictional product built as a design demo: the platform, the models, the numbers, the prices and the terminal output were all invented for it. Nothing is routed, nothing is stored, and no request leaves this page.
