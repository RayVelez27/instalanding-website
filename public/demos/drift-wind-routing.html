01 · The model

A pressure field, not a forecast PDF

Every system on the chart is a vortex with a radius, a spin and a strength. Advect a particle through the sum of them and you have the route — which is exactly what the hero is drawing, sixty seconds ahead of itself.

L
Fig. 01cyclonic

Read the systems

Lows spin one way and draw air in, highs spin the other and push it out. Radius and strength come off the pressure chart; nothing is hand-tuned per voyage.

H
Fig. 02advection

Advect the fleet

Each ship is a particle with a fuel curve and a laycan. We step it through the field the way the hero steps its 14,000, then keep the tracks that land on time.

Re-solve on arrival

Six-minute cadence. When a system deepens the route changes with it, and the bridge gets a heading, not a 40-page routing advice.

06 · Access

Put one hull on the field

We open a berth a week. Tell us the vessel and we'll route the next leg alongside your current provider, then show you both tracks.

Where does the field come from?

Global pressure and wind at 0.25°, re-gridded onto our own vortex model every six minutes. The hero on this page runs the same model at demo scale so you can see what the solver sees.

Is this a forecast or a routing advice?

Both, and neither is the deliverable. The deliverable is a track: waypoints and speeds your bridge can steer, with the field that produced them attached so a master can argue with it.

How does it reach the bridge?

API, email, or a one-page PDF over Inmarsat that fits in a 40 kB attachment. The board on this page is the same data, rendered for the office.

What happens when the field is wrong?

Every solve is stored with the field it used. When a system deepens faster than modelled, the re-solve arrives with a diff — not a new PDF that quietly replaces the old one.
