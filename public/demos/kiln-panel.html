Why a box

Some data cannot leave.

Three reasons a rack unit beats an endpoint, and none of them is that it is cheaper on day one.

Nothing egresses

The prompt, the documents and the output stay inside your network. There is no request to review, because there is no request.

Flat cost

A unit costs what a unit costs. The bill does not move because somebody shipped a chatty retry loop on a Friday afternoon.

It keeps working

No deprecation notice, no silent model swap, no provider incident. The weights on the drive are the weights you evaluated.

Inside the box

Four parts, no mystery.

Point at a part to find it in the chassis. Everything that wears out is replaceable from the front panel or the top lid.

K-04 · lid removed · air enters at the front and leaves at the rear

  1. 1

    Accelerators

    Four cards with 384 GB of memory between them. Enough to hold a large open-weight model whole, with room left for long contexts.

  2. 2

    Storage sleds

    Four hot-swap NVMe sleds, 30 TB in all, encrypted at rest. Pull the sleds and the weights leave with you.

    Turbine row

    Five fans pull air from front to rear. At normal load you hear the room, not the box.

    Power

    Two redundant 1,600 W supplies. Lose one and the unit keeps serving while you swap it.

Questions

Before you ask procurement.

Which models will it run?

Any open-weight model you hold a licence for, up to roughly 380 GB at the precision you choose on a K-04. Larger models run quantised, or across the eight cards of a K-08.

Does it ever need the internet?

No. Updates arrive as signed offline bundles that you load from a drive, so the unit can spend its whole life on an air-gapped network.

What happens when a part fails?

Drives, fans and power supplies swap while it runs. If an accelerator fails, the unit drops to three cards and keeps serving at reduced throughput until the replacement arrives.

Can several units work together?

Yes. Units on the same network pool into a single endpoint and share requests between them, and a model too large for one unit can be split across two.

How loud is it, really?

About 38 dB(A) at a metre under normal load, which is roughly a quiet office. Turbo is louder, and the red lamp on the panel tells you when it's on.
